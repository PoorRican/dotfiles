# Creative Ideation

A creative constraint gives direction to an open-ended request without deciding the project in advance. Match one to the person's interests and available resources, or choose one as a starting point; adapt it across media without overriding stated constraints.

Pick a constraint, randomly or matched to the person's domain and mood. Interpret it across media: a coding prompt can become a hardware project, an art prompt a CLI tool. Generate concrete ideas that satisfy it, explaining what to build and why each is interesting; include a rough effort and stack when useful. Respect the requested number and scope; choosing an idea does not itself authorize implementation.

## Constraint library

- **Solve your own itch:** Build the tool you wished existed this week.
- **Automate the annoying thing:** What's the most tedious part of your workflow? Script it away.
- **The CLI tool that should exist:** Think of a command you've wished you could type. Now build it.
- **Nothing new except glue:** Make something entirely from existing APIs, libraries, and datasets. The original contribution is how you connect them.
- **Frankenstein:** Take something that does X and make it do Y.
- **Subtract:** Strip a tool to its minimum viable function. Delete until only the essence remains.
- **High concept, low effort:** A deep idea, lazily executed. The concept should be brilliant; the implementation should take an afternoon.
- **Blatantly copy something:** Pick something you admire — a tool, artwork, interface. Recreate it from scratch. The learning is in the gap between versions.
- **One million of something:** Take a familiar thing to a scale where it becomes interesting.
- **Make something that dies:** A website that loses a feature every day, a chatbot that forgets, or a countdown to nothing.
- **Do a lot of math:** Generative geometry, shader golf, mathematical art, computational origami.
- **Text is the universal interface:** Build something where text is the only interface.
- **Start at the punchline:** Think of something that would be a funny sentence. Work backwards to make it real.
- **Hostile UI:** Make something intentionally painful to use.
- **Take two:** Remember an old project. Do it again from scratch without looking at the original.
- **Make a way to communicate:** Create a way to hold a conversation with someone else, not ordinary chat.
- **Asynchronous games:** Make something turn-based with no time limit; the game happens in the gaps.
- **One screen, two screen:** Take something associated with one screen and put it on a very different one.
- **Make a mirror:** Something that reflects the viewer back at themselves.
- **The useless tree:** Make something deliberately, completely useless.
- **Translate:** Take something meant for one audience and make it understandable by another.
- **Unexpected storage:** Save and open something using a medium not meant for storage.
- **Lists:** Itemizations, taxonomies, exhaustive recountings, iterations.
- **Animals:** Crab logic gates, fish playing the stock market, or another animal-inspired project.
- **An idea that comes from a book:** Read something. Make something inspired by it.
- **Go to a museum:** Project ensues.
- **NPC loot:** What do you drop when you die? What do you take on your journey? Build the item.
- **Mythological objects and entities:** Build the artifact.
- **Office Space printer scene:** Channel the catharsis of destroying the thing that frustrates you.
- **Borges week:** Something inspired by the library of Babel, or the map that is the territory.
- **Lights:** LED throwies, light installations, illuminated anything. Make something that glows.
- **Unexpected pixels:** Display an image in a medium not meant for rendering.
- **Make a self-portrait:** Be real or fake, in code, data, sound, or a directory structure.

For a useful idea, try an itch, a CLI, or automation; for something weird, try a hostile UI, Frankenstein, or the punchline. A learner can recreate something admired; a challenge might use scale, subtraction, or a second attempt. For a weekend, try glue or the punchline; for a visual idea, try math or scale.
