# Admission versus node fit

1. Find the Job and its owning Workload through owner references; inspect Workload conditions, reservation, admission and requeue state, and pod scheduling events. Deleted pods may leave useful events. Read the live PodsReady and requeue configuration instead of assuming timeouts.
   Inspect `status.conditions`, `requeueState` and `schedulingStats.evictions`; use owner references rather than guessing the Workload suffix. The PodsReady timeout clock starts at admission, not Job creation. After eviction, `Admitted=False/NoReservation` can mean quota was returned.
2. Separate Kueue flavor quota and reservations from scheduler node allocatable and effective requests of all bound pods, including unmanaged workloads. Admission does not guarantee placement. An idle GPU does not establish that CPU, memory or ephemeral-storage quota is available.
3. Classify scheduler events: insufficient resources indicate request fit; untolerated taints or selector mismatches identify different constraints. Check node conditions, taints, actual pod tolerations and flavor configuration. ResourceFlavor tolerations are injected into admitted pod templates and may explicitly tolerate node-condition taints; do not assume they cannot.
4. Compare each resource's headroom and workload request. Include init containers, overhead, sidecars and any enabled pod-level resources. For candidate releases, check whether each candidate frees enough of every binding resource; another arrival can consume it first. Missing usage metrics are not zeros. For sizing and history see the Kubernetes capacity evidence skill.
5. On a PodsReady timeout, reservation can be released and the workload requeued; inspect conditions and configured backoff rather than treating `NoReservation` as proof of a quota shortage. With a single scarce slot, blocked workloads can alternate holding it until timeout, making the other's insufficient-quota message a symptom rather than the original cause.
6. Disk-pressure taints can reflect node or image filesystem pressure that kubelet garbage collection cannot fix, such as external build caches. Inspect node conditions and filesystem usage; do not prune other users' caches without approval.

A completed step on a retry may be cached rather than freshly executed. Check the orchestration system's cache identity and artifact provenance before using it as proof of a fresh run. Changes to foreign workloads, quota or priority require appropriate authorization.
