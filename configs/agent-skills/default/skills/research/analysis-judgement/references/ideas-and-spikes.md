# Ideas and Spikes

Prompts for deciding whether a proposed change deserves investment and whether an experiment can answer what research cannot.

## Pressure-testing an idea

- Does the problem need solving? What is the boring alternative, compared on maintenance, reversibility, and blast radius? (src: brainstorm)
- Which claims about current behavior can be grounded in the codebase or other direct evidence, rather than assumption? Available evidence can lead the analysis instead of questions it already answers. (src: brainstorm)
- Is the question genuinely visual—would a mockup or diagram clarify it—or is it a conceptual choice that is better described in words? (src: brainstorming)
- The source skills disagree about mandatory approval, commits, and whether ideation may proceed to implementation; those conflicting workflow prescriptions are not carried forward. The discussion focuses on claims and alternatives, scaling design to the decision. (src: brainstorm; brainstorming)

## Choosing an experiment

- Can documentation or code reading resolve the feasibility question? A spike is useful only when a real experiment can reduce uncertainty. (src: spike)
- Which experiment has the greatest chance of invalidating the larger proposal, and can it run before easier, lower-risk parts? The experiments can be ordered by kill risk rather than convenience. (src: spike)
- What observable evidence would make the answer yes, partial, or no? A negative or partial result is useful when its constraints and evidence are explicit; an unobserved “it works” is not a verdict. (src: spike)
- Are comparison experiments testing the same question under comparable conditions, or are integration and data-handoff assumptions still untested? (src: spike)
