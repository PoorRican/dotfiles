# Experiment Log Interview

An interview exposes missing details and unsupported interpretations that can otherwise turn an experiment record into a confident but incomplete story. Adapt questions to whether the work is planned, completed, or revising an existing record; use `experiment-log-structure` for the artifact rather than imposing a template.

1. Identify the mode: a plan not yet run, a post-run write-up, or a revision. If unclear, ask which. For a revision, read the existing record before questioning; for a post-run write-up, preserve any genuine pre-registration.
2. Use `experiment-log-structure` and the project's record format. Ask one focused question at a time about consequential gaps, using the [question bank](references/interview-guide.md); avoid re-interviewing details already available.
3. Where a claim or assumption matters, name the assumption, ask its basis, offer one alternative or falsifier, and accept the response. Push for a comparison or missing diagnostic; record unresolved limitations if the information is unavailable.
4. Update the record as meaningful answers arrive. For a plan, stop with the pre-results material; for results, document what ran and how deviations affect interpretation. For a revision, record what changed, why, and which earlier claims are superseded.
5. Before finishing, check that evidence supports the interpretation, confidence fits the surviving threats, and implications and next steps connect to the broader question. Preserve any frozen plan and the revision history. Present the record location and its current state.

For consequential or ambiguous results, an independent skeptical reading can help: examine the evidence without the preferred interpretation, then bring its most important gap back as a question. The guide also provides alternative-explanation and calibration prompts.
